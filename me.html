Beyond what you see.

I am a curious person, with a constant desire to learn, to go deeper and to discover new things. I like to understand how things work, to explore ideas and to find solutions. It is an attitude I also find in my path in Computer Engineering, where curiosity becomes the wish to experiment, to challenge myself and to keep broadening my knowledge.

Alongside this more dynamic side, there is a side that loves tranquillity, nature and simple moments. I appreciate small details, sunsets, and the chance to dedicate time to the passions and interests that enrich my everyday life.

I believe deeply in authenticity, respect and loyalty. These are values I consider fundamental in relationships, and I try to keep them in the way I face my commitments and responsibilities. I think a person cannot be described only through their work, their studies or the results they have achieved: passions, interests and the way we interpret what surrounds us also help define who we are.

Technology and art

My interest in computer science lives alongside a strong curiosity for the world of art and the humanities. At first glance they may seem distant disciplines, but I believe they share a fundamental element: the ability to turn an idea into something concrete. Technology does it through logic, design and code; art through technique, creativity and personal expression. Both require study, research, precision and a good dose of imagination.

Among the artists I admire is Michelangelo, for his extraordinary technical mastery and his attention to detail. In The Creation of Adam, every element contributes to the composition, down to the famous space between the fingers. In Van Gogh, instead, I appreciate the ability to move beyond the conventions of his time and develop a recognisable style, born of a personal vision. Two different approaches that stand for two aspects I consider important in my own path as well: care in the making and the freedom to experiment.

Sport and discipline

Sport holds an important place in my life. The gym, in particular, has taught me that results do not come from a single moment of effort, but from consistency. They require patience, organisation and the ability to stick to your goals even when motivation fades.

It is an approach I also find in my studies and personal projects: moving step by step, learning from mistakes and improving over time. Because, whatever the field in which we test ourselves, I believe what makes the difference is above all the way we face the journey.

In the end, I believe that what we are does not depend on a single aspect, but on the whole of the experiences, choices, passions and values that accompany us. And this is exactly what is found beyond what you see.
